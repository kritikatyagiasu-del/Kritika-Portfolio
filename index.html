Data Migration for Digital TransformationInformation Technology

Combined SQL, Excel, Salesforce SOQL, Melissa address verification, Dun & Bradstreet DUNS data, and Power BI to transform 40 years of inconsistent data into one standard format across Salesforce and boundary systems.

$150M saved in consultant fees · 95% migration accuracy · delivered in 8 months

Role

Data Migration Product Manager, Data Architecture and Migration Strategy Lead

Objective

Increase business operations efficiency, reduce manual touchpoints, and reduce fulfilment time for customers.

Problem Statement

40-year-old data with no standard formatting within one system. The same data point was captured manually as a different metric across different systems, creating too many manual touchpoints — decreasing efficiency and creating friction with customer fulfilment. The data was unusable for training AI models to acceptable results.

Key Achievements

  • Completed the project from definition to architecture to deployment in 8 months
  • Saved the organization $150M in consultant fees, and a potential $100M in extended project timeline
  • Deployed the data migration with 95% accuracy
  • Standardized data across all platforms, enabling smooth AI training for new agentic AI projects

Impacted Users

  • GTM org users — AE, SE, CSM, SOMs, etc.
  • Marketing users — BDRs, MDRs, etc.
  • Services org users (SEM), Pricing & Deal Desk Managers, Contract Managers
  • Finance users — Planning, Accounting, Order Specialist; Revenue Recognition org
  • Partner org users — PAMs, PADs, POMs, etc.

Challenges

  • Inconsistent data format across systems; multiple entries for the same record
  • No standard data migration tool, limited budget and resources
  • Available resources lacked experience with large-scale data migration
  • No standard tools for deduplicating customer data

Trade-Offs Made

  • Budget constraint — manual SQL queries instead of migration tools, with 2 resources
  • Priority constraint — traded off finance data migration over services and other systems
  • Time constraint — defined CRM as source of truth instead of building a new MDM platform

Solution Approach

Delivered via the workflow below — defining retention rules and metrics, exporting and standardizing data across Finance and Salesforce, deduplicating accounts, importing into Salesforce, integrating boundary applications, and validating through QA/UAT before production deployment.

Data migration business flow — Plan → Extract → Standardize → Integrate → Deploy

Internal development teams across 18 business system teams.

Achieved data consistency from quote in CRM to invoice in ERP by automating product and pricing from CRM and generating invoices from ERP via third-party integration and internal triggers.

Role

Product Manager, Integration Strategy Curator

Objective

Create a consistent quote-to-cash process and eliminate customer data redundancy from manual ERP entry, with CRM as the source of truth for customer data.

Problem Statement

Data inconsistencies in customer name, address, and product pricing between the customer-approved quote in CRM and the generated invoice from ERP led to customer mistrust and rework for both business and IT teams.

Key Achievements

  • Completed the project from definition to architecture to deployment in 6 months
  • Improved customer trust and contract signing turnaround time by at least 2 weeks
  • Saved 2 days per order in manual data entry
  • Eliminated duplicate and redundant data across two business systems, creating a source of truth for future reference

Impacted Users

  • Account team — GTM org users (AE, SE, CSM, SA, etc.)
  • Finance users — Planning Managers, Order Specialist

Challenges

  • No single product master — CRM maintained its own products and pricing rules, while ERP's product master came from an external data warehouse
  • 3 different systems maintained by 3 different teams for the same set of data

Trade-Offs Made

  • Maintained the CPQ product structure over matching Oracle, to pre-plan future projects
  • Prioritized core products architecture over services products, trimming scope to avoid unacceptable timeline expansion

Solution Approach

Made CRM the source of truth for all customer data, product master, and pricing rules, and integrated CRM with ERP via a third-party application (Informatica) to replicate customer-approved quotes into orders, contracts, and subscriptions in CRM and invoices in ERP.

Resources Used

Internal development teams — 2 developers for CRM, 2 for ERP, 1 for integration — plus 1 Project Manager and 1 Product Manager/Owner
